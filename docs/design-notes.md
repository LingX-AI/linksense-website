# Design adaptation

The marketing site takes its visual direction from the public [OpenBot homepage](https://open.bot/), inspected on November 19, 2026. This replaces the earlier Exo Ape and Raycast direction. LinkSense retains its own branding, Inter typeface, media, product claims, and localized routes.

| Area | LinkSense adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | The centered heading, compact capability strip, open-source link and paired primary actions sit above a floating product composition. The full-width background uses the React Bits [GradientWaves](https://reactbits.dev/backgrounds/gradient-waves) canvas with a blue palette derived from the Figma logo and light/dark variants. The canvas stops out of view or in a hidden tab, and stays still with reduced motion. The competitors remain in the supporting copy rather than taking up extra heading lines. |
| Workspace | The restored floating composition places a plain task description beside a live DOM workspace, with two white deliverable cards on the right. Both the inline canvas and complete expanded demo keep the source proportions. The product link plays the authored 28-second introduction with sound. |
| Sections | Below the hero, three visual chapters group task-to-deliverable examples, team capabilities with a compact video entry, and deployment with the closing actions. Supporting introductions sit beneath their headings, content keeps a common outer edge, and surfaces identify chapter changes. The deployment controls are shared by the homepage and self-hosting; the homepage uses its own overview illustration. |
| Header and theme | A floating navigation row gains a surface while scrolling and becomes a native modal menu on narrow screens. The scheme follows the system by default; a 44px toggle persists an explicit override. Older browsers without `light-dark()` retain the light presentation. |

## Media

- The header/footer lockups, favicon, and social sharing card use the original blue L logo vectors from the current LinkSense Figma file. See [brand source and adaptations](brand-assets.md) for the exact node, SVG paths, and light/dark variants. The original blue is `#1866E1`, with neutral ink `#1B1D20`. The team showcase and closing action do not repeat the logo as decoration.
- All rendered product screens are exact PNG exports from the current [LinkSense Figma design](https://www.figma.com/design/GIy53t29UhECq6RxUx2CbA/Linksense?node-id=1-44868), exported November 20, 2026, and compressed to WebP at their native 1440×900 size. The homepage, feature sections, use-case applications image and product gallery use only these exports. See [asset provenance](../public/media/figma-assets.md) for the individual frame IDs.
- The homepage product video links in the hero, team workbench and supporting entry open the same authored MP4 tour with local music and synchronized sound effects, using the same player and localized title. See [video source and reproduction](product-video.md).
- The hero runs a real DOM example: it types a request, shows progress and delivers a workbook and report. The inline example repeats after a short pause on the completed files. Pointer or keyboard interaction keeps the visitor's state until an explicit replay. Visitors can edit a task, inspect the process, open file previews and replay it. Single selection is shared between project and recent-task entries. The floating files open their matching preview in the complete demo. Desktop columns have 40px gutters, with the request arrow centered in its gutter so the label, arrow and workspace remain distinct. The inline and full demos keep the 1440×900 source proportions. The user selected the earlier composition with plain task text, one slightly tilted workspace and two separate white file cards. The outer workspace now floats through a 19px vertical range with a slight roll. Text input holds it steady; focusing playback controls no longer freezes it. The playback controls use the original flat footer inside the workspace: expand on the left, a small outlined circular play key, quiet text label and 28-second timecode in the center and pause on the right. The open-source line sits 8px farther below the main hero actions. The rejected raised dock and standalone toolbar have been removed. The entire workspace and footer float together. Deliverable cards retain their bright surface on hover and gain a colored edge, title and arrow with a soft raised shadow. The open-source star uses bright yellow (#ffd43b) in both themes. The trial blue request tag and Q3 cover have been removed. Delivery motion pauses offscreen, in hidden tabs and behind dialogs; reduced motion keeps a completed, still example. The video and interactive demo remain separate actions, both with Escape, backdrop dismissal and focus return. The introduction video loops while open and retains its audio and native playback controls.
- The team workbench combines method sharing and tool connections into keyboard-operable tabs and retains its original blue surfaces. The decorative L icon, connector header and oversized background monogram are removed at the user's request. The two choices align to the top of the controls column, while the learn-more entry sits at its bottom, creating two distinct groups with flexible space between them. Each mode pairs its exact Figma screen with a foreground fan of illustrative capability cards. Each icon uses a pale 10% tint of its category color for the tile and the full category color for its stroke: violet skills, orange knowledge, teal plugins and brand-blue apps; the tools mode uses blue mail, orange documents and teal MCP. These colors are local to the icon tiles, with no changes to card surfaces or layout. Switching modes deals the cards and settles the screen once, with a reduced-motion static alternative. The screenshot carries an explicit play action and opens the same product introduction as the hero; the learn-more link retains the relevant case or connector documentation destination.
- The use-case section is a desk of three equal-width task folders. Choosing a folder changes its surface and check state, with a small vertical lift on its straight, aligned deliverable paper: a supplier comparison and brief, a weekly report, or an answer with a source. On desktop pointer hover or keyboard focus, the folder rises 8px while its paper pulls out by 14px and grows by 2%, with a deeper shadow and a 5px diagonal workflow arrow shift. The 320ms eased response reverses when the pointer and focus leave. Selection does not resize the grid. Comparison figures are labeled as illustrative, and all three links retain their localized workflow destinations. Narrow layouts expand one folder at a time; touch and reduced-motion layouts retain the static selection feedback.
- The five major headings below the hero share the existing H2 type scale (40px desktop, 28px mobile). The request-to-result, use-case, team and deployment introductions share one centered axis. The use-case link sits directly below its introduction instead of floating at the opposite edge. A white surface with a thin top boundary starts the content below the hero. At the user's request, the example request and files keep an open, unboxed layout on this white surface, with a maximum width of 65rem and no tinted background or surrounding border. Both example columns align at their top labels; the connecting arrow remains vertically centered. The team workbench remains the primary visual on its neutral chapter surface. Its supporting video is one compact, fully clickable playback card, with a 52rem maximum width. A blue play disc, smaller H3, original duration/caption note and text action share one row on a panel surface. The separate English poster preview and the section-wide separator are removed. Phones retain the play disc, title and metadata with a small trailing arrow. The existing video dialog is unchanged. Deployment then returns to white, with the diagram and controls aligned in an open two-column layout.
- Homepage rhythm is scoped to `.home-flow`: 80px chapter padding, 48px within-chapter separation, 40px heading-to-content gaps, 32px panel insets and 12px title-to-copy gaps. Tablet values are 64px, 48px, 32px, 24px and 12px; mobile values are 56px, 32px, 24px, 20px and 12px. Supporting copy uses 16px type with 1.65 leading; folder summaries and links use 14px. Mobile intro lines are balanced, and team choice descriptions remain at least 14px. The inner example title retains its smaller heading role. This refinement leaves the hero and navigation unchanged.
- Deployment and the closing action retain their original open white surface. A 48px desktop gap and a fine separator connect the deployment content to the closing row, with the heading on the left and the product/GitHub actions on the right. Phones stack and center the closing content. The headline keeps the confirmed 40px/28px scale. The experimental colored bands and curve artwork are removed at the user's request. The removed decorative logos and black model-endpoint label remain as separately requested changes.
- The homepage deployment illustration has no logo or brand-name hub, as requested. Worker and shared-capability nodes share an equal visual weight inside a thin deployment boundary, with a separate black model-endpoint label beneath it, using white text and iconography at the user's request. The illustration uses localized labels and scalable HTML/SVG geometry, with a maximum width of 30rem. The self-hosting page uses its own layered deployment diagram and page-scoped stylesheet. Capability choices stack on small phones so their full descriptions remain readable.
- The hero background is the vendored GradientWaves canvas from the React Bits registry, recolored for the current LinkSense brand. It is kept as JavaScript with a local type declaration; the shader itself is not type-checked. No OpenBot images or client code are shipped.
- Deliverable illustrations use example data. The previous blue signal artwork (`signal.webp`) and the two square menu artworks are no longer referenced by the current pages.
- Capability cards use one Lucide icon family: Workflow for skills, LibraryBig for knowledge, Puzzle for plugins and LayoutGrid for apps. White 1.8px strokes sit on solid brand-blue badges, with the same treatment for connected tools. The existing fan layout and interaction are retained.
- The bundled Inter font includes its [OFL license](../public/fonts/Inter-LICENSE.txt).

- Self-hosting is scoped to `.self-hosted-page`: the opening uses the exact homepage H1 scale (48px maximum for CJK, 50.4px for Latin), the existing H2 scale (40px desktop / 28px mobile), 18px supporting titles and 16px body copy. White deployment boundaries with a single pale-blue backing plane replace the former blue slab and dashed enclosure. The external model endpoint stays black, without a decorative logo. Core and Full share one comparison surface, aligned documentation actions and an explicit Core-plus-knowledge composition. Runtime controls align with a compact security-boundary note. Chapter spacing follows the homepage at 80px desktop, 64px tablet and 56px mobile. Existing copy, deployment guides, security destinations and theme behavior are retained.

- Use cases use a centered opening and one continuous three-part scene directory. A featured procurement example pairs its task with an accessible comparison table and companion brief; reporting and knowledge examples share aligned columns with distinct document treatments. The final handoff retains the original Figma applications export. Existing localized copy, illustrative-data notices, Full requirement and anchor destinations are preserved. Page-scoped spacing is 80px desktop, 64px tablet and 56px mobile. All colors inherit the current theme tokens; paper motion uses transforms only and is disabled for reduced motion and touch pointers.
- Homepage, use-case and self-hosted H1s share the homepage rules, including language, short-desktop and small-phone variants. Verified Chinese values at 1280×900 are 44.48px / 52.4864px leading / weight 650 on all three pages; at 320px width they are 26px / 30.68px / 650. Homepage typography values are unchanged. The use-case implementation passed Impeccable's source detector, TypeScript, ESLint, production build, 21 existing tests and the 18-route localized SSR check. Browser inspection covered desktop and 320/375/414/768px layouts with no case-content overflow, plus the light desktop and dark phone surfaces. Physical-device touch and screen-reader testing were not performed.
